A scheduled ride to campus02
Problem & research03

A free seat is only useful if you can board.

In our bilingual survey, 17 written incidents described full or unreliable buses. A missed pre-exam review carried a different cost from a slow trip home.

The sample
46 U-M students answered in English or Chinese, October 3–13, 2026. 35 had no regular access to a car.
A limit
41 of the 46 were graduate students. I would recruit more undergraduates before treating this as a campus-wide pattern.

My Lean Canvas
A gas share gives hosts a reason to return. A booking fee or Semester Pass could fund the service. I would track matches, on-time arrivals and repeat bookings on both sides.
My trust tests06

Trust runs in both directions.

I ran trust tests 4 and 5 to see what each side would accept as proof.

Test 4 · Riders
4 of 6 would book with a verified stranger. Promising, but too few to establish demand.
Test 5 · Hosts
3 of 6 would upload a license and insurance. Our upload assumption fell short.
Design response
We proposed third-party verification so hosts could prove eligibility without OnRoute storing their documents.
Team decision07

Reserve before the bus is late.

We chose campus commutes over grocery and airport trips. Classes recur, and a missed exam is costly.

The pivot
8 of 12 routes overlapped. We prioritized recurring bookings for time-critical trips. Actual paid demand still needs a pilot.

Outcomes & reflection09

From stated intent to real rides

The prototype supports search, booking, trip changes, and host posts. On-time arrivals and repeat hosting still need field testing.

What I learned
A rider wanting reassurance and a host wanting privacy are both expressing a need for trust. I would test those needs together earlier.
What I would do next
Run a paid, manually matched pilot on one recurring route. Record the requested arrival time, actual arrival, unmatched requests, and repeat bookings on both sides.
